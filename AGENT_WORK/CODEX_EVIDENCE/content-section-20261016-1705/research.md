## Confirmed record and timeline

According to the Ministry of Defence's October 15 announcement, the combined contracts are approximately ₹4,106 crore. GSAT-7C accounts for ₹2,563 crore with New Space India Limited for the Indian Air Force; Varunastra accounts for ₹1,543 crore with Bharat Dynamics Limited for the Indian Navy. The two component values sum to the announced total. Contracts were signed in New Delhi on October 15. [PIB announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2323814&lang=2&reg=48)

Defence Watch describes GSAT-7C as an indigenous satellite for secure, resilient communications, and Varunastra as a ship-launched anti-submarine torpedo developed by NSTL. These descriptions agree with the official account. Coverage on October 16 makes the subject current, but does not change the signing date. [Defence Watch](https://www.defencewatch.in/amp/story/ministry-of-defence/mod-signs-4106-crore-contracts-for-gsat-7c-satellite-and-varunastra-torpedoes)

## Context, significance and uncertainty

**Analysis:** the orders address complementary needs: coordination through military communications and the ability to engage submarines from ships. Domestic suppliers connect the capability plans to India's defence-manufacturing policy. This interpretation is supported by the functions described in the announcement; it is not evidence of immediate delivery, readiness or combat effectiveness.

The reviewed announcement does not establish delivery schedules, torpedo quantities, satellite orbit/transponder specifications, exact weapon range or deployment dates. Omit those details from the draft. Claims about jamming immunity, warhead size or operating depth in an AI-generated secondary brief were not adopted. Multiple outlets repeating a press release are separate coverage, not independent verification of technical performance. Distinguish “contract signed” from “equipment delivered or operational.”
